We publish figures drawn from technical documents, and technical documents get revised, misread and occasionally superseded between our reading them and your reading us. Errors are inevitable. What is not inevitable is hiding them.

## What we correct

**Substantive errors** — a wrong figure, a misstated specification requirement, a misattributed measurement, a wrong revision number, a claim about a certification tier that the standard does not support, a mischaracterised product. These are corrected as soon as we confirm them, and logged below.

**Superseded facts** — a standard is revised, a manufacturer changes a specification, a certification listing is updated. We update the article, change the `updated` date, and add a note explaining what changed and when. This is not an error on our part, but you are entitled to know the ground moved.

**Typographical and grammatical fixes** are made silently. We do not log a missing comma.

## How a correction appears

When we correct something substantive:

1. The article is amended, and the amended passage is accurate as of the new date.
2. A dated correction note is appended to the foot of that article, stating what was wrong and what it now says. We do not delete the record of the error.
3. The correction is listed on this page.
4. If the error was in a key takeaway — the part answer engines and search results quote — we treat it as high priority, because a wrong takeaway propagates well beyond this site.

We do not quietly edit a number and leave the article looking as though it always said that.

## How to report an error

Email [editor@panelproof.com](mailto:editor@panelproof.com) with `CORRECTION` in the subject line, or use the [contact page](/contact/). Please include the article URL, the specific passage, and — if you have it — the source you believe is correct. A link to the relevant standards document or measurement is the fastest possible route to getting it fixed.

You do not need to be polite about it. We would rather be corrected rudely than be wrong quietly.

## Response times

We aim to acknowledge within two business days and to resolve within five. A correction that affects a figure people may act on takes priority over everything else in the queue.

## Retractions

If an article's central claim proves unsupportable, we retract it. A retracted article stays at its URL with the original text struck through and a dated explanation at the top. We do not delete the page — that breaks inbound links and hides the record.
